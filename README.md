# Corner Cutters

An arcade F1 race in the HD-2D look: start lights, laps, positions, a minimap and results, against up to nine AI cars. Mobile-first portrait layout with a touch control deck.

**Circuits:**
- **Crescent Park**: anticlockwise over the hills, traced from the circuit on the edge of the big city between two continents (outline data from the same source as Silver Heath's, its main straight eased straight for the pit lane and its bends opened out, all but the heavy stop at Turn 12): the plunging downhill left of Turn 1, the climb through the fast bends to the hairpin, the long climb to the famous Turn 8, a four-apex left on the hillside at the top, the esses down off it, the long back straight, the stop at Turn 12 and the climb up onto the main straight; a lap of about 10,100 px, 30 s. (Its records from before it was retraced are let go, as for any circuit that changes.)
- **Silver Heath**: clockwise and fast, with a hairpin and a tight complex as the places to lift; a lap of about 31 s. Its outline is traced from circuit outline data by [bacinger/f1-circuits](https://github.com/bacinger/f1-circuits) (MIT).
Grandstands line each main straight behind the start line, and on the circuits in the country (Crescent Park, Silver Heath, Royal Park, Ardennes) they stand round the outside of the bends too, behind the gravel and the tyre walls, facing the apex: each with its own roof colour and flags waving on top, never so tall they hide the track from the camera (`src/f1/stands.ts`).
- **Harbour**: a street circuit after the most famous one of all, clockwise through a harbour town, its corners in order (eased apart so the track fits between the walls; the outline after the same data): the main straight beside the harbour with the pits on the harbour side, a right at the first corner, the climb up the hill, a long left and a right across the square at the top, down to a tight right and the slowest hairpin of all, two rights to the sea front, the long right-curving tunnel, the chicane at its exit, a fast left along the quay, the pool's left–right and right–left, the tight right at the bottom and the last right onto the straight; a lap of about 28 s. Steel barriers a step off the kerbs all the way round (pavement between, no gravel), a rich old town packed right up to the barriers (stone and ochre houses with arched, shuttered windows, here and there a balcony, under terracotta or flat roofs, some with battlements, and stone towers with tiled spires or battlements; tall, but never so tall that one hides the track from the camera), the casino above the hairpin (copper-green domes, a pillared porch, a garden with a fountain and palms), an open-air swimming pool at the foot of the pool section with swimmers doing lengths, a clay tennis court beside the climb with a rally on (each right behind the barriers beside a stretch of track that runs across the screen, where the camera, which shows little either side of a phone, has room to show it: `landmarksOf` in `src/f1/town3d.ts` settles each into the best-seen spot near where the layout puts it, turning the pool or the court a quarter turn if that's better; each must be at least half in a phone's picture at once as you drive by (a quarter of the big, tall casino), and if nowhere near the layout's spot is, it goes to the nearest spot round the lap just behind the barriers that is), the sea with a marina where the quay is best seen from the track (`src/f1/marina.ts`: three wooden pontoons running out from the quay, yachts moored stern-to along both sides of each, motor yachts and sailboats with their masts, bobbing on the water), three bigger boats going round out on the water with white wakes and superyachts at anchor further out; all to scale with the cars (an F1 car is 30 px, 5.5 m: the yachts 10–15 m, the boats going round up to 18 m, the superyachts 18–27 m), and the tunnel's see-through roof with its lamps. Easy on tyres: in 5 laps a stop pays for some and not others, so track position counts.
- **Royal Park**: after the temple of speed, clockwise through an old royal park, its corners in order (eased for the arcade handling): the longest straight of all (a quarter of the lap), the first chicane (a hard right, then left: the hardest stop), the long right of the big curve, the second chicane (left, right), the two rights of the woods, the straight under the trees, the fast left–right–left chicane, the back straight and **the banking**: a half circle of a right onto the main straight, the track tilting up toward the outside (a grade of 0.3, flat up to its inside edge, easing in and out over 240 px), on concrete run-off with seams running round it, up to a concrete wall and catch fence; a car on it is pushed toward the inside, as on any banked bend (`banking` in the layout; heights in `src/f1/circuit.ts`). **The floating podium** (`podiumDeck` in the layout): a deck hanging out over the main straight from a tower behind the garages, just past the line, with the podium's steps on it, red fascia and glass rail. **The park's trees** (`park` in the layout, `src/f1/forest3d.ts`): broadleaves in summer greens, a dark cedar here and there, in groves over the lawns and lining both sides of the woods, from their two rights along the straight under the trees to the fast chicane; by the forest's rules (clear of the track, the pits and the stands, and never tall enough to hide the track). A lap of about 30 s.
- **Ardennes**: the longest lap of all (about 13,600 px, 42 s), clockwise, up and down through a forest in the Ardennes, traced from the most famous circuit in those hills (outline data from the same source, its three tightest bends opened out so the track's edges never cross, and a few medium bends eased so a player on the touch stick, with no brake pedal, can live with the AI): the tight right of the hairpin at the top, the plunge past the pits to the bottom of the valley, then the steepest climb anywhere (a grade of 0.18) through the left–right–left at its foot, on up the long straight to the top of the hill, the right–left chicane and the right after it, the long right of the second hairpin, down through the double-apex left, the right–left in the woods and the two rights at the far end of the valley, the long, flat-out climb back through the left kinks, and the right–left of the last chicane onto the straight; 116 px from the top of the hill to the bottom of the valley. A set of tyres lasts three laps here (as on Silver Heath, wear runs by the second, so the long laps wear tyres less per second), so a 5-lap race needs one stop at most. **The forest** (`forest` in the layout, `src/f1/forest3d.ts`): thousands of spruces and firs, with here and there a broadleaf turning gold and rust, packed all round beyond the barriers on a dark forest floor and on out past the edge of the map; each only as tall as it can be without hiding the track from the camera, counting the hillside (a tree on the slope above a stretch of track has less room than one below it), and none on the pits, the garages or a grandstand. Drawn in instanced chunks, so those off screen aren't drawn.
- **Alpine Ring**: a short lap (about 8,400 px, 26 s), clockwise, on a mountainside, traced from the short circuit in the Austrian hills (outline data from the same source, its hairpins opened out so a player on the touch stick is as quick through them as on the wheel): the climb to the uphill right of Turn 1, on up the long straight to the hairpin at the top, down the back straight to the heavy stop at Turn 4, the sweeping lefts and rights down the valley, and two fast rights onto the straight; 70 px from the top of the hill to the bottom. In the forest, like the Ardennes.
- **Twin Lakes**: anticlockwise, round a bowl between two lakes, traced from the circuit in the big Brazilian city (outline data from the same source, its curving main straight eased straight for the pit lane and its tight bends opened out): down from the line into the left–right of the esses, the long curving back straight down to the lake, the twisty climb through the infield, the dip, the slow left onto the climb, and the long uphill drag to the line; a lap of about 7,900 px, 24 s. Its two lakes (`lakes` in the layout, `src/f1/lakes.ts`): one along the outside of the descent to Turn 4, one beyond the Curva do Sol, each right behind the barriers on a narrow sandy shore, flat in the hollows of the ground, with the grandstands kept out of them. A touch easier on tyres than Crescent Park. A gopher lives there (`gophers` in the layout, `src/f1/gopher.ts`): now and then one pops up out of its hole in the grass by the track, up the road from the camera, looks about, and scurries across to its hole on the other side (only with no car near; caught on the track by one, it bolts for the nearer hole, three times as fast). Scenery only: the cars never touch it.
- **Oasis**: clockwise, in the desert, traced from the circuit in the sands of the Gulf (outline data from the same source, its hairpins opened out and two stretches eased apart): the long main straight to the heavy stop at Turn 1, the long run to Turn 4, the esses, the hairpin, the downhill left of Turns 9 and 10, the back straight and the long right onto the straight; a lap of about 8,900 px, 28 s. Sand all round (`desert` in the layout: smoothed and striped on the run-off, rippled beyond the barriers, the gravel traps a redder brown so they stand out), palms (`src/f1/forest3d.ts`: clumps lining the circuit just past the barriers, where you see them as you drive by, and groves out over the sand; each a curved trunk and a crown of drooping fronds round its dates, by the forest's rules: clear of the track, the pits and the stands, and never tall enough to hide the track), camels (`src/f1/camels.ts`: six caravans of three to five, nose to tail under bright saddle blankets, walking slowly round long loops on the sand just behind the barriers, pacing as camels do, clear of the track, the pits, the stands and the palms). Easy on tyres, so a stop in 5 laps is a choice.
- **Caspian Shores** (id `baku`): a street circuit, anticlockwise through the old city on the Caspian, traced from the one there (outline data from the same source, its right-angled corners and the castle section opened out a little so the cars fit): the right-angle lefts and rights of the new town, the narrow, twisting climb through the castle section under the old city walls to the top of the hill, round the old town and down to the sea front, and the longest run flat out anywhere, curving along the sea front back to the line; a lap of about 10,900 px, 33 s. Its landmarks (`src/f1/town3d.ts`, placed by the same rules as Harbour's: behind the barriers, seen as you drive by, never hiding the track): **Qız Qalası**, the Maiden Tower, big, in the old town inside the walls, by the stretch out of the castle section onto the sea front, a round tower of banded sandy stone with its buttress, slit windows and battlements, on a paved square; and the **Flame Towers** on the hill to the north-west, three tapering towers of blue glass, the tallest at the back, flames of light running up them as their LED skins show; and **the Crescent** past the line, by the run to the hairpin: a tall crescent of blue glass standing on end, its horns up, on its plaza (it and the Maiden Tower may hide the barriers and the run-off's edge on the camera's side, never the racing surface: `HIDES_RUNOFF`). Along the castle section run the **old city walls** (`castleOf` in `src/f1/town3d.ts`): banded stone with battlements, a round tower every 130 px or so and a square gate tower in the middle, just behind the barriers on the inside of the castle section, round the old town, and unbroken: where there's no room right beside the track (inside a tight bend) the wall runs straight on to where there is. That puts them on the camera's side of the track at the top of the climb, so they're kept low enough that the camera sees the racing surface over them (they may hide the barriers and the run-off's edge there, and so may the Maiden Tower behind them: `seeSurfaceOver`); the town's houses keep clear of them. Walls all the way round, the old town packed up to them, the pit lane on the town side of the straight (AZERBAIJAN across its garages' roofs, in blue over a band of the flag's blue, red and green: `pitRoof` in the layout, `roofLetters` in `src/f1/circuitScene.ts`; barriers along its entry and exit roads past the garages, as on every circuit: `barrierTiles` in `src/f1/circuitScene.ts`), and the Caspian across from it, only a quay between the sea and the barriers all along the sea front as far as the line, where the shore turns away and the land round the hairpin is the Crescent's (its marina and boats, as at the Harbour; no house stands anywhere it would stand out over the water), and no tunnel (a street circuit's `tunnel` is now optional).
- **Nippon** (id `suzuka`): the figure of eight, traced from the one in Japan (outline data from the same source, its tightest bends opened out a little): down the main straight into Turns 1 and 2, up the snaking esses to Dunlop, the two rights of Degner, under the bridge, the hairpin, 200R up to Spoon, the back straight over the bridge, flat out through 130R, the chicane and onto the straight; a lap of about 10,200 px, 31 s. A car under the deck, hidden by it from the camera, is drawn through it as an outline (its edges, white; yours gold: `src/f1/outline3d.ts`). Drawn as an asphalt deck with white edge lines, concrete sides and unbroken walls (following the deck round its bends, where the walls that stop the cars are), and pillars clear of the road beneath; the circuit card's 3D line lifts the back straight over the crossing too. Round the crossing the eight's two halves run a strip of grass apart, with a row of tyre barriers down the middle of it (on any circuit, two stretches whose run-offs meet get one: `SPLIT` in `src/f1/circuit.ts`), and the deck's walls are a tile and a half thick, so no gap is left where the deck runs at a slant across the grid; and should a car get across anyway it gains nothing: a car's progress round the lap only ever follows it along the track (`stepProgress` in `src/f1/racing.ts`), so it's still counted where it left the track, and the lap doesn't count, till it comes back round to it. Spring there: the cherry trees in blossom (`blossoms`: groves of them over the grass and clumps lining the lap, `src/f1/forest3d.ts`), their petals falling, drifting onto the track on the breeze and lying there (some from the start, under the trees lining it): on the track till a car kicks them off, lap after lap; on the grass a while, then gone; a car driving over them kicks them up, out from under it and along in its wake, the faster the higher, to come down again a way on (`src/f1/petals.ts`: in the race and behind the menu).
- **Glacier Pass** (id `glacier-pass`): clockwise over a mountain pass, a circuit of our own (`GLACIER_PASS` in `src/f1/layouts.ts`): from the valley floor down the long main straight to Turn 1, a fast right–left to string the field out, then up the switchbacks, three hairpins stacked up the mountainside, each two tight right angles (the heaviest stops of any circuit); along the summit and over **the Crest**, a jump the cars fly off at full speed (about half a second in the air), then down off the mountain, back through the infield over **the Kicker**, a second jump, and the long sweep onto the main straight. - **Dust Bowl** (id `dust-bowl`): clockwise on dirt, a rallycross circuit of our own (`DUST_BOWL` in `src/f1/layouts.ts`): down the main straight into a fast right, through the twisty section (a tight right, a left and the hairpin), the long back straight over the jump, the kink and the sweeping right onto the main straight; a lap of about 6300 px, about 21 s, the shortest of all. On dirt (`dirt` in the layout): every car on **off-road tyres** (`dirt` in `src/f1/tyres.ts`, OFF-ROAD on the tyre line, an earth-brown band round each tyre), the only compound there whatever the weather. Loose earth grips less than tarmac (80% of a slick's grip; in the rain it turns to mud, slower and slipperier still), so the cars slide through the bends, and sliding hardly wears the tyres; knocks and rubs that would hurt on tarmac don't (soft earth, hay bales: `DIRT_KNOCKS` in `src/f1/raceControl.ts`); with no choice of tyres there's no strategy (no TYRES row on the menu): a car stops only for worn tyres or repairs, for a fresh set of the same. Dirt everywhere: the track wet, dark earth, ruts worn deep along it, wet clods strewn over it and puddles in the ruts catching the light, a berm of drier earth along each edge (no white lines, no kerbs); a pit lane of packed earth; dry earth all round, the run-off graded in stripes and the ground beyond the barriers rougher, stones strewn over it; hay bales for the barriers; every car's tyres bigger and knobbly, their tread blocks caked in earth (`createCarMesh`'s `offRoad`), and mud flung up behind each car at speed, more of it sliding through a bend (`src/f1/race/drawCars.ts`). Monster trucks in the infield (`monsterTrucks` in the layout, `src/f1/monsterTrucks.ts`): an arena of two lanes joined by a hairpin at each end, a dirt ramp either side of a pile of crushed cars on each lane, and two trucks going round it half a loop apart, so they pass each other going opposite ways, both in the air over the wrecks at once: up the ramp, nose up off the lip, level at the top of the flight, down to meet the far ramp, squatting on their springs as they land, and round the hairpin to do it again. **Teams:** eleven, each in its own colours: Milk Energy, Prancing Monkey, Golden Arrows, Calrissian Racing, British Lime, Reneé, Frankie's Groove, Cheaper Milk, Deutche Motor Werke, MaaS and Grandma's Fave (`src/f1/teams.ts`), each with its own logo (`src/f1/logos.ts`), shown on the menu's TEAM row and under the start lights. So teams tell apart from afar, every car carries its team's pattern along the top (a stripe, twin stripes, bands, chevrons, halves, a split or a coloured nose; teams sharing a pattern have far-apart colours), and teammates by the T-camera on the air intake: dark on the team's first car, bright green on its second. Each team has two drivers, by their three-letter codes: Milk Energy VER and HAD, Prancing Monkey HAM and LEC, Golden Arrows RUS and ANT, Calrissian Racing NOR and PIA, British Lime ALO and STR, Reneé GAS and COL, Frankie's Groove ALB and SAI, Cheaper Milk LAW and LIN, Deutche Motor Werke HUL and BOR, MaaS OCO and BEA, Grandma's Fave BOT and PER. Pick yours on the menu's TEAM row; a race runs five teams of two: yours (you in the first seat, the team's second driver beside you) and four drawn at random. The second driver's car has the green T-camera. Your driver wears a shiny gold helmet (every other driver's is white), and on the grid your car is also marked by a gold ring on the ground round it and a gold arrow bobbing above it, until the lights go out. The results show each car's team.

**Damage and the safety car:** every car, yours and the AI's, takes damage from walls, landings and contact. A damaged car smokes, then burns, and loses up to 30% of its top speed; your health shows as five blocks in the readout. A wrecked car is cleared off the track and classified DNF. A wreck brings out the safety car: it joins ahead of the leader, the field queues behind it on a 200 px/s limiter with no overtaking (and nobody gets past the safety car itself), and after 12 s with the leader lined up behind it, it goes in. Passing a car under it costs 5 s a place, added at the flag. **Holding station is done for you:** your limiter follows the car you must stay behind (or the safety car itself), easing you in a gap behind it (72 px, about a car and a half of daylight) at its speed, and braking if you're well inside that gap and closing, so going flat out at the queue settles you into your place instead of into the car ahead or past it; the readout shows the limit it's holding you to (SC 200, falling as you close up). The same holds under the virtual safety car. A big crash a car survives (one hit taking 40% of its health: its nose, and maybe more, on the track) calls the **virtual safety car** instead (VSC): every car on a 190 px/s limiter where it is, nobody passing (5 s a place, as under the safety car) and no tow, but no car to queue behind, so the gaps hold; after 10 s (as long as the debris lies) VSC ENDING is called, and 3 s later the green. A wreck while it's out brings out the safety car in its place. The rules are in `src/f1/raceControl.ts`. **A big crash tears parts off** the car: its nose (with the front wing) every time, and a wreck loses a wheel or two as well. They fly clear the way the car was going, thrown outward and up (harder the harder the hit), tumble and bounce, come to rest (a wheel on its side), lie there a few seconds and sink away; a car that pits gets its parts back: fitted as the crew finish the stop, before it pulls away from its box. They fly on the race's clock (held while it's paused), and the replay after your flag throws them again just as they flew, the parts off the cars from the moment they came off (a wreck cleared from the track by then shows again too). Only for the picture: nothing hits them (`src/engine/debris.ts`, drawn by `DebrisLayer` in `src/engine/render/effects.ts`).

**AI racecraft:** the AI races the cars round it (`aiInput` in `src/f1/racing.ts`, tuned in `RACECRAFT`). Quicker than a car ahead on a straight, it pulls out and passes on the side with room, but only a pass it can finish before the braking zone (its speed advantage, the momentum it carries and the tow, against the straight left); it holds its line till it's a length and a half clear, and tucks back in if the straight runs out first. Caught on a straight by a quicker car still in line behind, it makes one move to cover the inside of the next bend (never across a car alongside). A car alongside isn't turned in on, and side by side into a bend the one a nose behind gives way. Off its usual line in a bend it takes the bend at the speed its radius there allows. None of it while the pack is still bunched from the start (the first 15 s) or under the safety car, except giving way side by side into a bend, which holds at the first corner too (not under the safety car). Each driver's racecraft comes from the difficulty (EASY 0.3, NORMAL 0.6, HARD 0.9, each driver a little either side): the higher, the bolder its passes and the harder its defending (on EASY it doesn't defend). The grid is mixed a little from the race's seed, as after a qualifying with its mistakes, so some quicker cars start behind slower ones and race their way forward.

**Racing incidents:** now and then an AI driver close behind another AI car into a braking bend misjudges it and dives in far too late, aiming for a gap that isn't there, hardly braking (`lunge` in `src/f1/racing.ts`). Its first contact is a racing incident (`racingIncident` in `src/f1/raceControl.ts`): both cars take a hard hit by how fast it closed, and the car hit spins round and slows, so it ends in a crash, debris, sometimes a wreck and the safety car, and the crash replay when it's near you. The chance per such moment goes with the difficulty (EASY 6%, NORMAL 4.5%, HARD 3%) and the driver's aggression (a charger more, a metronome less); AI drivers never dive at you. Over 20 five-lap races on NORMAL, 13 had AI cars touch, 3 a big crash and 2 a wreck.

**Driver personalities and mistakes:** each named driver races in a style (`src/f1/drivers.ts`): a CHARGER (bold, a little error-prone), a METRONOME (measured, rarely wrong), a VETERAN (canny and consistent), a DEFENDER (hard to pass) or a ROOKIE (keen, and the most mistakes). A style adds to the racecraft the difficulty gives, and sets how often the driver gets a bend wrong. Now and then, going into a braking bend, an AI driver makes a mistake: brakes too late (in too fast, then runs wide and gathers it up past the apex) or runs wide through it, each costing a few tenths and a chance for the car behind; more on EASY (8% a bend before style) than on HARD (2.5%), none while the pack settles or under the safety car. The dice come from the race's seed, so a race plays again the same. A mistake near you is called out: LOCK-UP · LEC, or HAD RUNS WIDE. The styles are one table, easy to change.

**Driver numbers:** each driver races with their number (`DRIVER_NUMBERS` in `src/f1/drivers.ts`; you take your seat's): on a white plate on the engine cover, as big as fits between the rear wheels, in blocky pixel figures so it still reads with the car small on the screen; small beside each name in the timing tower; in the results' and the Championship standings' NO columns; and in the grid pan's banner (P1 #4 NOR · CAL).

**Difficulty:** EASY, NORMAL or HARD, in the menu's SETTINGS and remembered (`src/f1/difficulty.ts`). It sets how much a crash costs every car (the damage from walls, landings and contact, and how much a damaged car slows) and how quick and closely matched the AI field is: EASY halves crash damage against a slower, spread-out field; HARD takes 1.75× the damage against a field at 99% of the racing line's pace, closely matched.

**Tyres:** every car's tyres wear as it drives: with speed, faster while sliding (a drift eats them) and on grass or gravel (`src/f1/tyres.ts`). Worn tyres grip and turn less and lose top speed, a little at first, then sharply past about 70% wear; gone, they cost about a fifth of a lap. A set of SOFTs lasts about two laps at its best, so races are 5 laps by default, long enough for a stop. The readout shows your tyres: the compound, blocks and the share left (past the cliff, WORN on a line of its own, under the values, so the readout keeps its width), in the compound's colour.

**Weather:** DRY, DAMP or WET, on the menu's WEATHER row and remembered (`src/f1/weather.ts`). Four tyre compounds for tarmac, each marked by a coloured band round every car's tyres, as in F1 (and off-road tyres on dirt: Dust Bowl): two slicks for the dry, **red SOFTS** (the quicker) and **white HARDS** (4% slower, lasting twice as long), **green intermediates** for a damp track, **blue full wets** for rain. The wrong tyre grips less and is slower (wets on a dry track also wear out fast), and a wet track is slower than a dry one even on the right tyres: in AI races, damp is about 5% slower and wet about 11%. The crews fit the right compound for the weather at the start and at every stop. Damp and wet races are overcast with a darker track, spray behind the cars and every car's red rain light blinking at the back; wet has falling rain. Lap records are kept apart for each weather.

**Dynamic weather:** a Quick Race's WEATHER row also has CHANGEABLE (Time Attack and Time Trial keep to the three, the same all session). A changeable race runs to a forecast drawn from its seed (`src/f1/forecast.ts`): SHOWERS (dry, a shower comes through, then it dries), DRYING (a wet start, the rain stopping early on) or LIGHT RAIN (dry, then rain that leaves the track damp). The track's wetness runs from 0 (dry) through 1 (damp) to 2 (wet): rain wets it (the harder the rain, the wetter and the faster), and once it stops the track dries, slowly. Each compound's grip, speed and wear are eased between its dry, damp and wet ones, so slicks are best below about 0.4, intermediates up to 1.5, full wets above. The sky, the light, the track's darkness, the falling rain and its hiss, the spray and the rain lights all follow the weather as it changes. When it turns, the banner says so (RAIN, THE RAIN HAS STOPPED, TRACK WET · FULL WETS TYRES) and so does the radio; on the wrong tyres a line under the tyres says BOX FOR INTERMEDIATES (or SLICKS, FULL WETS) and the engineer calls you in for the right ones (BOX FOR INTERMEDIATES). At a stop the crew fits the tyres for the track as it will be over the next half lap. The AI boxes for the right tyres too, each crew calling it at a slightly different loss of grip, so the field doesn't all stop on the same lap. Qualifying runs in the weather the race starts in. A Championship has no WEATHER row: each round has its own forecast, drawn from the season (mostly dry, now and then damp or wet all race, about one round in four changeable), shown on the Championship screen and the loading card. Changeable races keep their own records.

**Slipstream:** close behind another car at speed, you're in its wake: up to 6% more top speed, strongest right behind it and fading with distance (gone at about five car lengths) and off to either side. The tow builds over half a second and fades soon after you pull out, so pulling out to pass carries the extra speed alongside: the slingshot. TOW in the readout fills as it builds, with a rush of air. The AI is towed too, but uses the extra speed only while there's room ahead to brake from it. None under the safety car, in the pit lane or after the flag. The rules are in `src/f1/slipstream.ts`.

**Controls lap:** a new player's first launch opens a lap of Crescent Park on their own before the menu, with a prompt at a time for the controls on the device they're using (the touch stick, keys or a gamepad): go, full speed, slowing for a bend, drifting (it moves on after two bends without one), keeping inside the white lines, and on round to the line. Each prompt moves on once you've done it; a cut is explained (in a race: a warning, then +5 s). A skips it at any time, and once it's done A goes to the menu; either way it isn't shown again on its own. Anyone who has already played (records, a season, a circuit unlocked) goes straight to the menu. The rules are in `src/f1/onboarding.ts`.

**Championship:** CHAMPIONSHIP on the menu's first screen leads to the Championship screen. A season is a round on each circuit in turn (Glacier Pass and Dust Bowl aside: they're free, open to everyone from the start), with the team and qualifying picked on that screen (TEAM, CAR, QUALIFYING and LENGTH rows, up whenever a season can be started; mid-season, NEW SEASON brings them up). Win the title and the screen gives way to a celebration first (`src/f1/screens/titleWin.ts`): gold light rays turning behind a big trophy that drops in, WORLD CHAMPION stamped letter by letter, your team on a band in its colours, your points, wins and margin, fireworks bursting all over and three waves of confetti, a fanfare and a buzz; TAP TO CONTINUE (or A or START) to the standings. With reduced motion it's a still card. Qualifying is picked again before each round: mid-season a QUALIFYING row sits above RACE ROUND (kept with the season, and remembered for the next round) and the difficulty from the settings, against the same field all season: your team and four others, each driver with the same pace every round. Points as in F1 for the top ten (25, 18, 15, 12, 10, 8, 6, 4, 2, 1; none for a DNF), and the drivers' standings by points, then by the best results. Between rounds the screen shows the rounds raced (your place in each), the standings (points, wins, last result) and RACE ROUND n with the next round's FORECAST; after the last round it crowns the champion. A round counts once you've seen its results (A or START); leaving it before then doesn't count it, and a finished round can't be restarted. **Circuit unlocks:** only the first circuit is open for a Quick Race, a Time Attack or a Time Trial at first; each later circuit unlocks once a Championship reaches it (you've raced the round before it), said on the Championship screen. A locked circuit is dimmed on the menu (LOCKED · REACH IT IN A CHAMPIONSHIP). Anyone who already had a record on a circuit keeps it open, and `?circuit=` opens any circuit (for testing). The season is kept on the device after every round, so it can be picked up later; NEW SEASON (pressed twice while one is running: the first press brings up the rows) starts again. The rules are in `src/f1/championship.ts`.

**Time Attack:** beat the clock (`src/f1/timeAttack.ts`). On your own from the same standing run-up as a Time Trial, on tyres that don't wear; at the line a countdown starts with a little more than a sector's par on it, and each checkpoint you pass (each sector's end, the line included) adds that sector's par (a third of the AI's reference lap) times a factor that's generous on the first lap (×1.1) and shrinks each lap after (0.04 a lap, to ×0.6), so the clock wins in the end; EASY is a little kinder, HARD a little meaner. A cut across a marked corner costs 3 s. The banner shows the clock (red in its last 5 s) and +time at each checkpoint; the readout the clock, how far you've got and your best. At TIME UP the session holds on how far you got (laps and sectors) and whether it's a new record (your best on each circuit and weather is kept, and shown on the circuit card); A or START goes again. Driven by the reference player at Crescent Park on NORMAL: a steady player gets 1 lap and 2 sectors, a good one 4 laps and 1, a very good one 7 laps.

**Time Trial:** a Time Trial is flying laps on your own, from the same standing run-up as qualifying, on tyres that don't wear. Each lap is timed from the line; a cut across a marked corner deletes it. At each sector the banner gives your split against the lap you're chasing: purple when it's quicker than your record, green when it's quicker than your best this session, amber when it's slower. Your record lap on each circuit (and in each weather) is kept on the device as a ghost: a see-through car that drives it again beside you from the line, with the live gap to it (GAP, green ahead, red behind) in the readout. START goes back to the run-up; SELECT back to the menu. The rules are in `src/f1/timeTrial.ts`. **World boards** (`src/f1/boards.ts`, `src/f1/screens/worldBoard.ts`): a new lap record of yours goes on the circuit's online board in that weather (dry, damp or wet), under your initials (the Daily Challenge's); kept on the device and sent later without initials or a connection. Once it's on, the banner says where you stand after the record's announcement: WORLD BOARD · #12 OF 340 (WORLD RECORD for the top). The circuit screen in a Time Trial shows the circuit's board in the weather picked: the top three and your place, with a field for your initials until you have them.

**Medals and the trophy cabinet** (`src/f1/medals.ts`): on each circuit a Time Trial lap and a Time Attack run earn BRONZE, SILVER or GOLD. A lap as quick as the quickest AI car on EASY is bronze, on NORMAL silver, on HARD gold (shares of the AI's reference lap, flat out on its own); a Time Attack run as far as a driver lapping at NORMAL's or HARD's pace gets is silver or gold, and a full lap bronze. The readout shows the next medal and what it asks for (GOLD ● once it's yours; in a Time Attack, passing a medal's distance mid-run moves it on to the next, so past bronze it shows silver's); a new medal is stamped onto the screen (in a Time Attack, at TIME UP): it drops in big, lands with a bounce, a glint runs across it, and it fades after a couple of seconds; the circuit card shows the medal you hold in that mode. TROPHIES, under SETTINGS on the menu's first screen, is the cabinet: a trophy for the Championships you've won and a tally of gold, silver and bronze, then two tabs (tapped, or left/right on the deck): MEDALS, each circuit's Time Trial and Time Attack medals, popping in one after another, and ACHIEVEMENTS, in two columns so the whole cabinet fits on a phone's screen. Winning a Championship puts a glowing trophy on its screen, dropped in to a burst of confetti the first time (`src/f1/screens/celebrate.ts`). With reduced motion asked for by the device, they just appear, without confetti. Medals and titles are kept on the device (the save's `trophies`).

**Achievements** (`src/f1/achievements.ts`): twenty-four things to do, each unlocked once and kept on the device: CHEQUERED (finish a race), PODIUM, WINNER, GIANT KILLER (win on HARD), RAIN MASTER (win in the wet), FROM THE BACK (win from the back half of the grid), CHARGE (gain five places), HAT TRICK (pole, the win and the fastest lap), SPOTLESS (no damage and no track-limits warnings), PURPLE PATCH (the race's fastest lap), ENDURANCE (a race of 15 laps or more), BALD (finish on tyres worn down to 0%, as the readout shows), TORCH (finish with your car on fire), ROCKET START (a GREAT LAUNCH), TOO KEEN (jump the start), TORPEDO (hit three cars or more in the first 8 s after the lights at the Ardennes), BOX, BOX (a pit stop), CAN'T STOP WON'T STOP (finish a race without a pit stop), LAPPED (lap a car), SCRAPHEAP (wreck your car), GOLDEN (a gold medal), GOLD STANDARD (a Time Trial gold on every circuit), CHAMPION and GLOBETROTTER (race on every circuit). The race's are judged at your flag, the rest as they happen; each one slides down from the top of the screen as a toast when it's unlocked. The trophy cabinet's ACHIEVEMENTS tab lists them all with how many you have, those unlocked in gold and the rest dimmed with what they take (medals and titles won before achievements are filled in when it opens).

**Qualifying:** turn QUALIFYING ON on the menu (it's off by default, and remembered) and the race weekend starts with one flying lap on your own. You start standing on the straight before the line, with about 5 s of driving to get going and get your bearings (TIMING STARTS AT THE LINE); the lap is timed from the line (FLYING LAP) back round to it. A cut across a marked corner deletes the lap (LAP DELETED) and the next one is timed afresh. Your time goes up against the AI's (each worked out from a reference lap on the racing line and the driver's pace, a little either way), and the table sets the grid in time order: pole to last, no time at the back. A or START goes to the grid; A during the lap skips qualifying and you start mid-grid, as with it off. Restarting a race (START, or RESTART on the pause screen, or START on the results) runs it again from the same grid, without qualifying again; to qualify afresh, restart during qualifying or pick the circuit again. The rules are in `src/f1/qualifying.ts`.

**Balance:** `tests/balance.test.ts` measures the difficulties headless with a reference player (`src/f1/referencePlayer.ts`) driving through a player's own inputs (the touch stick, which has no brake pedal, or the wheel). On a flying lap the quickest AI car matches a very good lap on HARD and a good one (95% of the racing line's speed) on NORMAL; on EASY it's slower than a steady player (90%). In a 5-lap race from mid-grid a good player wins on EASY by seconds, a steady one misses the podium on HARD, and a very good one races at the front on HARD (over four races, on average in the top four). Change the AI or the cars and these tests say whether the difficulties still hold.

**Blue flags** (`src/f1/blueFlags.ts`): a car about to be lapped (a car most of a lap ahead of it in the race within 140 px behind it on the track) is shown the blue flag and lets it through: an AI driver moves over to the side away from it, lifts a touch on the straight while it's close and doesn't defend, and the car lapping it goes by rather than queueing behind (in a couple of seconds). Shown to you, it's BLUE FLAG · LET HAM BY in blue and your engineer on the radio; shown to a car you're coming up to lap, BLUE FLAG · MOCHI; the timing tower marks a car under blue flags. Not under the safety car or the virtual one, in the pit lane, while the pack settles from the start, or after the flag.

**Track limits:** the kerbed bends are marked corners, each with a yellow-and-black strip just off the inside at its apex. Cut across one with all four wheels past the inside edge (on the kerb, or two wheels over, is fine; so is running wide on the outside) and it's a strike, one per time through the corner: the first two are warnings (TRACK LIMITS · WARNING 1/2, in amber, with a buzz), and every cut after adds 5 s to your finish time, in red. LIMITS in the readout keeps count. The rule applies to every car, but the AI keeps to the track. Against the clock (a qualifying lap or a Time Trial lap) the rule is the strict one: all four wheels past the white line anywhere, on either side, a cut or running wide, deletes the lap (LAP DELETED · TRACK LIMITS), even in the very step it crosses the line; turning off onto the pit entry road doesn't count. The rules are in `src/f1/trackLimits.ts`.

**Sector lines:** a white line across the track where each sector after the first starts (the start/finish line, chequered, is the first's): where the Time Trial's splits are taken and the Time Attack's checkpoints are (`sectorStarts` in `src/f1/racing.ts`).

**Pit stops:** each circuit has a pit lane beside its main straight, wider than the track (a fast lane, and a box for each team beside it, the garages behind), behind a pit wall. Long entry and exit roads branch off the track and rejoin it, smoothly, 360 px each way, with the boxes between the two speed-limit lines; the whole lane lies on the main straight. **The shortest pit lane** is the two roads and the boxes' run, 1,040 px (`PIT_LANE_MIN` in `src/f1/pits.ts`), and every circuit's main straight is at least 1,120 px (`PIT_STRAIGHT_MIN`, tested), so a lane never bends round a corner: Crescent Park, Silver Heath and Ardennes were lengthened along their straights for it. Leave the track on the pit side at the entry and you're committed: the car drives itself down the entry road, slowing (not until it's off the track) to reach the first line at the 120 px/s limit, down the lane on the limiter, into your team's box while the crew fits new tyres and repairs its damage (1.2 s, plus up to 3 s for a wreck's worth of repairs; a team's second car queues behind its first), and back out to the second line and up the exit road to speed, handing it back where it meets the track's edge (an AI car keeps to that edge along the blend line, 400 px, before crossing to the racing line). Cars in the pits keep behind each other by how far down the lane each is (side by side on a bend, each knows which of them is ahead). The pit wall calls **BOX, BOX** on the way to the entry when a stop now pays (worn tyres, damage, or both, against the laps left), and the AI stops on the same sums. Passing a car in the pits under the safety car is no penalty. The rules are in `src/f1/pits.ts`.

**Replay:** a few seconds after you take the chequered flag, the race holds and your finish plays again: the 10 seconds up to just past the line, every car where it was (the safety car too), the camera on your car, slowing down as you cross the line, under a blinking ● REPLAY. A skips it. Then the champagne ceremony follows. Every car is recorded 20 times a second through the race, the last 15 seconds kept (`src/f1/replay.ts`).

**Crash replays** (`CRASH_REPLAY` in `src/f1/replay.ts`): a big crash of yours (a wreck, or a single hit taking 40% of the car's health, the safety car's own measure) or an AI car wrecked within sight of you is replayed: 1.5 s after it (seen live first), the race holds, ● REPLAY, and the 4 s round it play again on the crashed car, at 0.8× and slowing to 0.3× through the impact, with the parts thrown again as they flew, and each car as it was then (each frame records whether it was whole, smoking, burning or wrecked: a car on fire now is whole before its crash, and catches fire when it did); then the race goes on from where it held. A (or the phone's back button) skips it. In a race, before your flag, and never within 20 s of the last.

**Grid pan:** before the start lights, the camera glides down the grid from pole to the back, a moment on each car, with its grid place, driver and team on the banner (yours in gold); then it swings back to your car and the lights come on. A skips it straight to the lights. Nothing moves and the lights wait while it plays. GRID WALK in the settings (SKIP) leaves it out every race: straight to the lights.

**Final lap and the flag:** as you start your last lap the banner calls FINAL LAP, with a bell, and the lap panel says FINAL LAP until you finish. From the moment the winner crosses the line a chequered flag waves on the pit wall at the line (`src/f1/flag3d.ts`), for every car that finishes after; and as you take yours, a big waving chequered flag shows over the picture for a few seconds, with the fanfare.

**After the flag:** the banner says where you finished (FINISHED · P3), your finish replays, and then the champagne ceremony follows: the race is finished at once (anyone still racing gets the finish their pace would bring, and everyone is put back in their team's garage, nose out, side by side with their teammate), the top three in parc fermé. A (SKIP) cuts straight to the ceremony; A skips the wait for the others after a DNF too. There's no in-lap to drive and no parking on the straight; the cars that have finished cool down round the lap and into their garages.

**Daily Challenge** (`src/f1/daily.ts`, `src/f1/screens/daily.ts`): one Time Attack a day, the same for everyone in the world.
- **The day's challenge:** a circuit and weather drawn from the date (UTC), always on NORMAL. The circuit is never the same two days running, and over a few months it's every circuit; the weather is dry about two days in three, damp some days, wet now and then. Any circuit can come up, locked ones included.
- **Runs:** as many as you like; your best counts. A run is how far you got before the clock ran out, and two runs as far are split by how soon the last checkpoint was reached.
- **The leader's ghost:** each run is recorded, and your best goes to the board with it (ten frames a second, a few kilobytes). Every run races the day's leading run as a gold ghost, from the clock starting: CHASING ZED · 34 CP when it's loaded. (A run over ten minutes goes on without one.)
- **Its screen:** today's circuit and weather, when the next challenge comes, your best today, your streak of days played, and the day's online board (the top ten and your place, out of everyone who's run it). It also has your three initials for the board (typed into one field, letters and digits only, which brings up the phone's keyboard; asked for the first time, CHANGE after, and PLAY waits for all three; while it's being typed in, the keys are its letters, not the deck's), PLAY and BACK.
- **At time up:** the banner says your best today and your place on the board (TIME UP · 2 LAPS + 1 SECTOR / NEW BEST TODAY · #12 OF 340). SHARE puts your place on the card. AGAIN goes again, and EXIT goes back to the challenge's screen.
- **On the device:** your best on each day, your streak, and a best not yet on the board (sent the next time there's a connection). A run that passed no checkpoint isn't sent.
- **The board:** Supabase, through `submit_daily()` (it keeps each player's best) and `daily_board()`. Without a backend the screen says the board isn't set up, and the challenge still plays.

**Play stats** (`src/f1/metrics.ts`, `src/engine/backend.ts`; setup in [`supabase/README.md`](supabase/README.md)): anonymous counts of how the game is played go to the game's Supabase project:
- the game launched (with the platform, web or android, and the build's version);
- a session started and finished (its mode and circuit, your place);
- the km you drove (at a scale of 0.67 m to a pixel, so a lap of Silver Heath is the 5.9 km of the circuit it's traced from);
- a result shared;
- the team and driver picked for each session;
- the time in the game: each stretch while the page is showing, summed per launch, sent as the page is hidden or closed.

A player is a random id the device makes for itself; nothing else about them is sent. Events are queued and sent every 20 s, and as the page closes. STATS in the settings (SHARE or OFF) turns them off, and it's only there when the build has a backend. Without one (`VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_ANON_KEY` unset), nothing is sent and the game plays as ever. The totals are on the **Pit Wall dashboard** (`stats.html`), published to GitHub Pages by `.github/workflows/pages.yml` (https://theaob.github.io/corner-cutters/; setup in `supabase/README.md`), or from `select public.game_stats();` in the project's SQL editor. The schema (`supabase/schema.sql`) lets the public key add events and nothing more.

**Reports** (`src/f1/report.ts`): REPORT, on the pause screen in a race and in the top right corner of every menu screen (the modes, the circuits, the Championship, the Daily Challenge, the shop; also under DONE in the menu's settings, for a controller), takes a screenshot of the game as it is (the pause screen out of the way; the 3D picture copied as it's drawn, `src/engine/render/capture.ts`, with the HUD over it, drawn with html-to-image, loaded only then), and opens it to draw on with a finger or the mouse (four pens, UNDO and CLEAR) over a box for what happened. SEND sends it to the game's backend: the picture to the private `reports` storage bucket, the words, the circuit and mode, the build's version, the screen's size and a smaller copy of the picture (960 px) to the `reports` table (`supabase/schema.sql`). The dashboard's REPORTS section shows them, newest first, behind a code of your own (`set_reports_code` in the SQL Editor; `supabase/README.md`). An error nothing caught in the game goes to the play stats too (`src/f1/crashes.ts`: its message, where, its stack and the screen; once a launch each, at most 10; none with STATS off), shown under ERRORS on the dashboard with the same code. While it's open the game's controls are its, not the race's or the menu's; CANCEL or the back button closes it.

**Sharing your result** (`src/f1/shareCard.ts`, `src/engine/share.ts`): SHARE, centred under the results table after a race (and under the banner after a Time Attack, and under the standings on the Championship screen once a round's been raced), makes a square card of how you did. A Championship's card (`seasonCard`) shows your place in the standings after the rounds raced, your points, wins and podiums, and once the season's over the title (CHAMPION between gold cups) or your podium. It shows the game's name between chequers, the circuit and the mode, your place big in the middle over the circuit's map (gold, silver or bronze on the podium; a Time Attack's distance in words, 5 LAPS over + 1 SECTOR on two lines when there's a sector in it, between two pixel cups in its medal's colour), WINNER, PODIUM or the gap, three numbers (your grid slot, the places you made, your best or the fastest lap), your team in its colours, the day and where to play. It goes to the phone's share sheet with a line to go with it ("I finished P1 at Crescent Park in Corner Cutters 🏁 Can you beat it?", and the link). In the Android app that's the system sheet (`@capacitor/share`, the picture written to the app's cache first); in a browser, the Web Share sheet where there is one; otherwise the picture downloads and the line is copied (the button says SAVED · TEXT COPIED). The link is `VITE_SHARE_URL` at build time (default `theaob.itch.io/corner-cutters`).

**The champagne ceremony** (`src/f1/podium3d.ts`, designed in the Podium Lab): parc fermé. The top three cars stand nose to the camera on a patch of tarmac in front of a wall in the winner's team colours, with the circuit's name and GRAND PRIX across it. Each car has a gold, silver or bronze board with its place at its nose: the winner is in the middle, second on its left and third on its right. Each driver stands up on their car in their team's colours (yours in the gold helmet), and under each board a name plate gives the place, the number and the name. The ceremony runs 10 s, in beats:
- The drivers climb up.
- At 2 s they lift their trophies, the confetti starts falling and the winner hops.
- At 4.5 s the corks pop (with a sound, and foam bursting out), and the champagne sprays: second and third at the winner, the winner at both. Fireworks burst over the wall.

The camera holds on third (its plate readable), sweeps across to the winner, pulling back, and settles on the three of them, centred. It's a set of its own: the camera sees only it while it's on (the set and the lights on a layer of their own), so no grandstand stands in the way, and the readout, minimap, lap panel and timing tower are hidden on any screen. The champagne song, "Triumphal Fanfare", plays. The results follow at 10 s, or at A.

**Controls:** each device drives the way it suits, and the game follows whichever you used last:
- **Touch:** the analogue thumbstick points where you want to go, and how far you push it is the throttle (full from about 85% of the way out). B drifts. The stick floats: put your thumb down anywhere on its side of the lower half of the screen and it comes to it, centred under it; let go and it glides back to its corner, where it rests dimmed (`floatOffset` in `src/engine/deck.ts`).
- **Keyboard:** the car itself: ↑ (or W) is the gas, ↓ (S) the brake (held once stopped, it reverses), ← → (A, D) steer, X or Shift drifts.
- **Gamepad:** the right trigger is the gas, the left the brake, the left stick steers (gently near the centre), A drifts, Start pauses, Y restarts, Back is SELECT and the d-pad works the menu.
- **DRIVING** in the settings (`src/f1/driveStyle.ts`) picks the way on any device:
  - **AUTO** (the default): each device as above.
  - **POINT**: the car goes where you point on every device: the touch stick, the arrow keys as eight ways (let go to slow), or a gamepad's left stick, pushed further for more speed.
  - **STEER**: you drive the car itself on every device; on the touch stick, up is the gas, down the brake, and across steers.

  The controls lap's prompts follow it.

**Between screens** (`src/f1/screens/curtain.ts`): a dark curtain fades in before a screen goes and out once the next is up, so the menu, a race and the Championship screen follow one another instead of cutting (the page opens with it down, lifted once the first screen is up). Going to a race it carries a loading card while the circuit is built: its outline and name, the mode (a Championship's round) and the weather, and a run of chequered squares lighting in turn. A race's results open with your race at a glance, five boxes in a row, each with its icon: where you finished (in gold, silver or bronze on the podium; OUT in red), your grid slot and the places you made, the gap to the winner (or your time, if you won), your best lap (purple if it was the race's fastest) and your pit stops (with any penalty, in red). Under them the table slides in a row at a time, each with the places it gained (green ▲) or lost (red ▼) from its grid slot, the fastest lap in purple, its pit stops and any penalty by their icons (a wrench, a warning triangle), and the records under it each by a cup. **The readout's icons** (`src/f1/race/icons.ts`, `readoutView.ts`): each line has a little pixel icon in its own colour (a stopwatch for LAP, a lap arrow for LAST, a star for BEST, a cup for REC, the car, a tyre, the tow's air, a warning triangle for LIMITS, a ghost for GAP, a wrench for BOX, an hourglass and a flag in a Time Attack, a medal for the next medal), and its icons, labels and values each line up in a column of their own (a grid: the pixel font isn't monospaced); qualifying's table slides in too; and the Championship's standings slide in with the points the last round brought (+25, +18…) beside each total as it counts up to them.

A message across the race (the banner: the controls lap's prompts, BOX BOX, the safety car…) that takes two lines takes even ones, never a word on its own: a message in phrases (UP TO GO · LEFT AND RIGHT TO STEER) a phrase a line, otherwise balanced by the browser, or split at its most even point where the browser can't (`evenLines` in `src/f1/race/banner.ts`). The game opens on a title splash (`src/f1/screens/splash.ts`): the menu's backdrop race behind it, the logo (CORNER over a red-and-white kerb over CUTTERS, ARCADE GRAND PRIX under it) dropping in, TAP TO START (PRESS ANY KEY without a touch screen) blinking under it and the build's version at the foot. A tap, a key or a gamepad's button starts, and the theme plays from there (browsers allow no sound before one); the curtain drops and the menu comes up, or a new player's controls lap. A link straight to a race or a mode (`?circuit=`, `?mode=`) skips it. On a keyboard, up/down moves, left/right changes the circuit or a row, Enter picks or races, and B goes back. In the Android app the phone's back button goes back as you'd expect (`src/engine/backButton.ts`): in a race it pauses (or skips a crash replay), on the pause screen it resumes (closing its settings first), after the flag it leaves (a Championship round counts once its results are up); on the menu it goes up from a mode's circuit screen, the settings or the trophy cabinet to the modes, and from the Championship screen to the menu; and on the menu's first screen it says PRESS BACK AGAIN TO EXIT, a second press within 2 s leaving the app. On a keyboard: X or Shift is B, Z or Space is A, Enter is START, Backspace is SELECT, Esc or P pauses, and V switches between the handheld and wide layouts (as SCREEN does in the settings: HANDHELD or WIDE, on a device with a mouse or trackpad, or already wide; phones don't get it).

**HUD:** the readout at the top left has your lap, last, best and record times; the gaps to the cars either side of you (`▲ RAZZ +0.84`, `▼ MOCHI −1.20`), timed at points round the lap as timing loops do (`src/f1/gaps.ts`); your car's health and tyres. Gaining or losing a place lights up your position in the strip (on a phone, your row in the timing tower) for a moment (green ▲, red ▼); a new fastest lap of the race is called in purple, and shown in purple in the results.

**Sound:** synthesised with Web Audio, so there are no sound files (`src/engine/audio.ts`, `src/f1/sounds.ts`): your engine, built as an engine sounds (a V6's firing pulses and the crank's other orders in one waveform, two banks a touch out of tune, a soft clip for grit and a breath of exhaust noise; climbing through eight gears to about 12,500 rpm, dropping back less at each upshift in the high gears, with the crack of the ignition cut, grittier on the throttle, popping and crackling on the overrun when you lift), the nearest rival's engine (louder as it closes, higher as it comes and lower as it goes), tyre squeal while sliding (a wavering tone over a hiss), a rumble on grass and gravel (and a grainy crunch on the gravel) and a buzz over the kerbs, rain hiss in the wet, a thud for each hit (bigger for a harder one) with the scrape of metal as the sparks fly, **the crowd** (a murmur when you're near the grandstands, swelling into a cheer heard round the circuit at lights out, at each car you pass, at your flag, and a gasp at a crash), the pit limiter's beep between the speed-limit lines, **the team radio's** click and squelch, a beep for each start light and a higher one at lights out, a fanfare at your flag, a chime for a lap record or your fastest lap, and menu clicks. Browsers only allow sound after a tap or a key, so it starts with the first one; pausing, or leaving the app or tab, silences it. SOUND in the settings sets the volume: OFF, LOW, MEDIUM or HIGH (remembered).

**Music:** an anthem for the landing screen (the menu): the game's theme song, "Triumph in Motion" (`public/music/theme.mp3`), the race's, "Relentless Momentum" (`public/music/race.mp3`: two minutes, looped from its first sound at 0.19 s to its last, at half the level, under the engines), a calmer one for after the flag and a march for the podium, looping, crossfading when they change: the race's gives way to the calmer one at your flag, and when the ceremony starts the champagne song, "Triumphal Fanfare" (`public/music/podium.mp3`), takes over, on through the ceremony and the results. (The theme's placeholder, should its file not load, is an original tune in the style of a cinematic racing TV theme: D minor at 124 bpm, a driving 16th-note synth pulse under a heroic brass tune, string pads, a pumping octave bass and big drums.) The champagne song's placeholder, should its file not load, is a march in the manner of a bullring parade, in the spirit of Bizet's "Les Toréadors": A major, dotted brass fanfares in thirds over an oom-pah tuba, a marching snare and cymbals. MUSIC in the settings sets its own volume (OFF, LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH; remembered); pausing, or leaving the app or tab, silences it with the sounds. The player is `src/engine/music.ts` and the tracks are listed in `src/f1/music.ts`. Until a composed track is in (the calmer one after the flag still plays its placeholder), each plays a placeholder: a chiptune loop written there as notes (the menu in A minor at 96 bpm, the race in E minor at 150 bpm, quieter under the engines, played should its file not load) and synthesised on the device, so there's nothing to download. **To drop in a composed track:** put the file in `public/music/` (AAC `.m4a` or `.mp3` play everywhere, iOS included) and set the track's `file` (e.g. `file: 'music/race.m4a'`); with an intro before the loop or an ending after it, set `loopStart` and `loopEnd` in seconds, and the loop is gapless. `gain` sets the track's level in the mix. A file that's missing or won't play falls back to the placeholder.

**Team radio** (`src/f1/radio.ts`): in a race or qualifying, your engineer comes on the radio for the moments that matter: BOX, BOX as the pit wall's call goes up, the safety car and the virtual one and the green, a good stop, the last lap, a purple lap, track limits (a warning or a penalty), a wreck, and how it went at your flag (a win, a podium, points or not). Each line is said a few ways, shown in a radio panel in your team's colour along the bottom of the screen, keyed with a click and a squelch; one line at a time, the next once it's done, a stale one dropped.

**Like on TV** (`src/f1/launch.ts`, `src/f1/tower.ts`): in a race, a timing tower under the minimap shows the top three, then the cars around you, each with its place, a bar in its team's colour, its name and its gap to the leader (+1L a lap down; PIT and OUT), you in gold. The start is yours to win or lose: once all five lights are lit, getting on the throttle before they're out is a jump start (+5 s at the flag, and your engineer tells you so; already on it as they light, and holding it, is just an ordinary start); after they're out, your reaction time is judged, and a quick one (0.25 s or less: a GREAT LAUNCH, 0.45 s: a GOOD one) shows it and gets you a better getaway. The AI cars have reaction times too, so the field leaves the line in a scramble rather than all at once: each about 0.16–0.42 s off the lights (quicker the sharper its racecraft, with a bit of luck), and now and then a bogged start (0.7–1.2 s; less often for a consistent driver), drawn for each car from the race's seed. Each place you gain or lose is called out (PASSED HAM · P5, or HAM PASSES · P6; not a car in the pits), and wheel to wheel with a rival the camera eases back a touch to show you both.

**Feel** (`src/f1/shake.ts`): a hit shakes the camera (a knock barely stirs it, a crash throws it about; a hard landing thumps), the kerbs judder it and the grass and gravel more, more at speed, and it fades by itself; a big hit (8 health at once, or a wreck) stops time for a moment, the race at a crawl for 70 ms, so the crash lands. SCREEN SHAKE in the settings turns it off (remembered). Sparks fly off every hit, a car's or yours (thrown back the way it was going), and from under a car that lands hard. Every car's red rear light comes on as it slows (braking, or lifting at speed: in F1 it's the hybrid harvesting), on top of its blinking in the wet, so you can read the traffic ahead. Near top speed and in a tow, pale streaks flow past the edges of the screen the way you're going (never over the middle, where the racing is) and the camera pulls back a touch.

**Vibration:** your car buzzes when it crashes (harder for a harder hit, long for a wreck) or lands hard, rumbles on grass and gravel, and patters over the kerbs (`src/f1/rumble.ts`); the deck's buttons tick under the finger. In the Android app it goes through Capacitor's Haptics plugin, in Android browsers through the Vibration API; iOS Safari has no vibration, so there it's silent. VIBRATION in the settings or on the pause screen turns it off (remembered).

**Records:** your best lap on each circuit, and your best race time there for each number of laps (penalties included), are kept on the device between races (`src/f1/records.ts`). Each lap is saved as soon as it's done. The menu shows each circuit's lap record, the race readout has it as REC, a lap that beats it is announced (NEW LAP RECORD), and the results show both records, marked NEW! when this race set them. Your best qualifying lap on each circuit is a record of its own, kept apart from the race's lap record (a flying lap on your own isn't a race lap, and a deleted one never counts): during qualifying REC shows it, and the qualifying table shows it, marked NEW! when you've just beaten it. Each circuit is fingerprinted (`src/f1/circuitHash.ts`: a hash of its shape, hills, pit lane, tyre wear, walls and banking, not its name or scenery), and the hash it had when you last played is saved: when an update reshapes a circuit (or a designer draft is edited), your records and Time Trial ghosts on it, in every weather, are forgotten, since they were set on another track. A circuit open only thanks to such a record stays open.

**Fixed-step simulation:** the race runs in steps of exactly 1/60 s, however fast or slow the screen draws (`src/engine/fixedStep.ts`): a frame runs as many steps as its time holds, and each car is drawn between its last two steps, so a 90 or 120 Hz screen still moves smoothly. A race comes out the same to the last digit on a 30 fps phone and a 144 Hz monitor, and the same as in the headless tests (which step at 1/60 s too); a frame too slow for 4 steps slows the game rather than stalling it. A race's random draws (the rival teams, the start-light wait) come from its seed (`src/engine/rng.ts`), so the same seed and the same driving play it again exactly: the ground for M3's ghosts and replays.

**Frame rate:** when a phone can't keep up, the quality steps down; if even the lowest level is no quicker, the browser is holding the page to 30 fps (Low Power Mode, or Safari throttling a game embedded in another site's page, as on itch.io), so it goes back to where it started. Embedded like that, the menu offers **PLAY IN ITS OWN TAB**, which isn't throttled; saves carry over.

**Race length:** a race is 5 laps (long enough that worn tyres make a pit stop pay). A Quick Race can be as long as you like: LAPS on the menu steps through 1, 2, 3, 5, 7, 10, 15 and 20 (remembered); a Championship season is a SPRINT (5 laps a round) or a GRAND PRIX (10), picked on its LENGTH row when it starts, so a season's rounds are alike (`src/f1/laps.ts`).

**Mid-race save** (`src/f1/raceSave.ts`): a Quick Race or a Championship round in progress is kept on the device as the game goes into the background, as you pause and as you finish each lap, so a long race survives the app being closed (or the phone killing it). Next time, RESUME RACE sits above the modes on the menu (the circuit and your lap under it, and the Championship's round); it builds the same weekend again (its seed and grid) with every car where it was, its laps, tyres, damage, strategy and pit stop, the weather and the safety car, and picks it up paused (RACE RESUMED). The kept race goes at your flag, or when you leave the race or restart it; a Championship round's is forgotten once the season has moved on, and any kept for over a week.

**Strategy** (`src/f1/strategy.ts`): in the dry, a race is a choice of SOFTS and HARDS and when to stop. Every plan with up to three stops (each stint on either compound, each stop at the end of a lap) is timed from the tyre model and what a stop costs, using the circuit's practice: one car's flying lap on new SOFTs, timed and its wear measured, once a circuit (a circuit hard on its tyres, like Royal Park, favours the HARD; on the streets of Harbour and Caspian Shores the SOFT runs a 5-lap race without a stop). The AI drivers each take one of the plans close to the quickest (half the field the quickest, the rest spread over the others), so a field splits: some on a one-stop, some on two, some starting on the HARDS; they stop on their plan's laps, for repairs once badly damaged (under 70% health) if that pays, and plan the rest again from the wear they measured after a stop off the plan. Your pit wall gives you the quickest plan, said once the race is under way (STRATEGY: SFT → SFT · 1 STOP · BOX LAP 3), and calls BOX, BOX on its lap; a Quick Race's TYRES row starts you on the plan's tyres (AUTO) or the SOFTS or HARDS. On the way into the pits, NEXT: SOFTS or HARDS shows under the banner, ◀ ▶ (or left and right) to change it. In the damp or the wet the weather decides the tyres, as ever.

**TUNE:** the button at the top right of a race (on a phone, at the bottom) opens sliders for AI opponents, an AI pace adjustment (× the difficulty's), camera zoom and look-ahead; changes apply at once and are kept on the device (Reset puts the defaults back).

**Kerbs** (`kerbed` and `KERB` in `src/f1/circuit.ts`): red-and-white bands just inside both edges round every tight bend, as whole kerbs: a gap under 64 px between two is filled, a sharp spot gets one at least 40 px long, and each runs on 16 px past its bend at either end. Their stripes are all 12 px long measured along the kerb itself (as long round the inside of a bend as the outside), each kerb starting on red, drawn as one continuous band (no gaps on a curve). The kerb tiles that buzz and rumble under you are the same stretches.

**Track designer** (a tool of its own, not part of the game: `designer.html`, `src/designer/`): It's hosted on GitHub Pages, under `designer/` on the same site as the stats dashboard (https://theaob.github.io/corner-cutters/designer/), redeployed by `.github/workflows/pages.yml` on every push to the main branch that changes it; there the game beside it (for DRIVE IT) sends no metrics, drives of a draft not being anyone's play. Locally, `npm run designer` opens it (`npm run designer:build` builds it into `dist-designer/`: the designer as its `index.html` and the game as `play.html`, so the folder can be hosted as it is; the game's own build never includes it). It shows a circuit as the game builds it, track, run-off, pit lane, grandstands and scenery, in 3D through a free first-person camera: hold the right mouse button to look about, W A S D to move, Q and E down and up, Shift to go faster, and F to switch between flying and walking at a driver's eye height over the ground. Start from any of the game's circuits or a blank, then left-click a control point (the white one is the start line) and drag it over the ground; N adds a point after it, Delete removes it, [ and ] lower and raise the ground there (Shift: five times as far), Home makes it the start line, G takes the camera to it, Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y undo and redo. The panel edits the rest: name, scale, the pit lane, tyre wear; what it stands in (parkland, a forest, the desert with its palm groves and walking camels, or a street circuit's town); for a street circuit, how far off the track its walls stand, a tunnel, a sea (ADD A SEA, then drag its blue corners), each landmark (the casino and its garden, the swimming pool, the tennis court, the Maiden Tower, the Flame Towers: tick one and drag its orange box where you want it; the game sets it where it fits, clear of the track and seen as you drive by) and old city walls along a stretch of the lap; lakes (ADD A LAKE, then drag its points); a bridge where the lap crosses itself (FIND THE CROSSING finds it and sets the bridge there, which stretch goes over swappable, and the checks say if the two stretches aren't at the same height where they cross); a banked bend; a podium over the straight. So a figure of eight like Nippon or a street circuit by the sea like Caspian Shores can be built from a blank. N and Delete add and remove a sea's or a lake's corners as they do the track's points. And the panel edits the picked point, corner or landmark, and checks the circuit as you go against what the game needs: its main straight long enough for a pit lane and the lane on it, no bend tighter than the kerbs allow, no two stretches of track on top of each other, no grade too steep; AI LAP drives an AI car round it on the real physics. DRIVE IT ▶ opens the game on the draft as it is, in a tab of its own (a quick race or a time trial alone): it races under an id of its own, `designer-draft`, so its laps never land in a real circuit's records, and the race's quit comes back to the designer (`src/f1/designerDraft.ts`; `npm run designer:build` builds the game beside the designer for it). COPY AS CODE gives the layout as TypeScript to paste into `src/f1/layouts.ts` (and add to `LAYOUTS`); SAVE JSON and OPEN JSON keep and bring back a layout as a file, and the draft is kept in the browser between visits. Heights are set per control point and written out as the elevation profile.

**URL flags:** `?circuit=crescent-park` (or `silver-heath`, `harbour`, `royal-park`, `ardennes`) goes straight to a race (add `&mode=timetrial` for a Time Trial there), `?mode=championship` opens the Championship screen, `?inputlog` lists the input events the page receives and the buttons held (for debugging controls on a phone), `?seed=<n>` plays races from that seed (the same rivals and start; `__cc.seed()` gives the current race's), `?debug` exposes `window.__cc` for tests (`__cc.look(x, y)` holds the camera on a point of the map, `__cc.landmarks()` lists a street circuit's landmarks, `__cc.stands()` the grandstands, `__cc.wreck(3)` wrecks the car in P3, `__cc.callVsc()` calls the virtual safety car, `__cc.debris()` counts the parts torn off and still on the ground, `__cc.toPits()` damages your car and puts it in the pit entry, `__cc.flag(1)` waves the chequered flag for everyone now with you in P1, to watch the in-lap), and `?desktop` / `?mobile` force a layout.

## Publishing to itch.io

`.github/workflows/itch.yml` tests and builds every push and pull request. A push to the default branch (or **Run workflow** in the Actions tab on it) also uploads `dist/` to itch.io with [butler](https://itch.io/docs/butler/) as the `html5` channel, versioned `<package version>+<commit>`.

Setup (once):
1. On itch.io, create the project with kind **HTML**. Under the embed options, tick **Mobile friendly** (portrait) and **Fullscreen button**, and set a viewport of about 390×844.
2. In the GitHub repo, under Settings → Secrets and variables → Actions, add the secret `BUTLER_API_KEY` (itch.io → Settings → API keys) and the variable `ITCH_TARGET` = `<itch-user>/<game-slug>`.
3. Push to the default branch. After the first upload, tick **This file will be played in the browser** on the upload in the project's edit page.

Until both settings exist, the workflow skips the upload and warns which one is missing.

## Android

The same web build, wrapped by [Capacitor](https://capacitorjs.com) into an Android app (`android/`, `capacitor.config.ts`): full screen, portrait, the screen kept on, played offline; the back button goes from a race to the circuit menu, then out.

`.github/workflows/android.yml` builds an APK (a download under the run's **Artifacts**) and uploads it to the itch.io page as the `android` channel, so it can be downloaded there on a phone. It runs on every push and pull request (and by hand: Actions → android → Run workflow); only a push to the default branch uploads to itch.io. To install, allow installs from your browser or files app when Android asks. Each build has a higher version code, so it installs as an update over the last.

**Signing:** the APK and the AAB are signed with your release key, from the repo's secrets: an Android app installs as an update over the last build only when both are signed with the same key. Without the secrets, a build is signed with the runner's throwaway debug key (the run says so with a warning): it installs fresh, but not over an earlier build. (No key is kept in the repo: the old sideload key that was is retired.) Create your key once and keep it safe (an update must always be signed with it):

```sh
keytool -genkeypair -keystore release.keystore -alias cornercutters -keyalg RSA -keysize 2048 -validity 10000
base64 -w0 release.keystore      # the value for ANDROID_KEYSTORE_BASE64
```

then add the repo secrets `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_BASE64`, `ANDROID_KEYSTORE_PASSWORD`, `ANDROID_KEY_ALIAS` (`cornercutters`) and `ANDROID_KEY_PASSWORD`. A build signed with your key won't install over one signed with the old sideload key (or a debug key): uninstall that once first (its save goes with it).

**The Google Play build: the Championship as an in-app purchase** (`src/f1/purchase.ts`, `src/f1/screens/shop.ts`). The game is free. Built with `VITE_STORE=play`, Crescent Park and the free Glacier Pass and Dust Bowl are open in Quick Race, Time Attack and Time Trial, and the Championship (and with it every other circuit, opened as a season reaches it, as before) is a one-time purchase through Google Play Billing (`cordova-plugin-purchase`). Its button on the menu reads *unlock: a season, and every circuit*, and picking it opens its shop: what it brings, the price from Google Play, UNLOCK, RESTORE PURCHASE and BACK; once bought it's kept in the save (`progress.championship`), and on a new install the store gives it back on start-up (or with RESTORE PURCHASE). Every other build has no store and everything open: the web game and the APK on itch.io. The workflow builds the Play version as an AAB alongside the APK (`corner-cutters-play-aab` under the run's **Artifacts**, signed with the release key when its secrets are set), for uploading to the Play Console by hand. In the Play Console: create the app (`io.github.theaob.cornercutters`), upload a first AAB (a test track will do), then add a one-time product with the id **`championship`** and its price, and activate it; purchases can be tried by license testers on an internal test track (a build installed from Google Play: a sideloaded APK can't reach Play Billing, and says GOOGLE PLAY ISN'T AVAILABLE HERE).

Locally (with the Android SDK and JDK 21): `npm run android:play` builds the Google Play AAB (`android/app/build/outputs/bundle/release/app-release.aab`); `npm run android:apk` builds `android/app/build/outputs/apk/release/app-release.apk`. `npm run android:art` redraws the launcher icons and splash screens (`tools/android-art.py`, needs Pillow).

## YouTube Playables

`npm run youtube:build` builds the version for YouTube Playables into `dist-youtube/`, checks it against Playables' rules and zips it as `corner-cutters-youtube.zip`, ready to upload in the Playables developer portal (once YouTube has accepted the game: the Playables interest form). `.github/workflows/youtube.yml` does the same on every push to main and keeps the zip as the run's artifact (Actions → youtube → the run → Artifacts).

- **The SDK:** the build (`VITE_STORE=youtube`) loads YouTube's Playables SDK (`https://www.youtube.com/game_api/v1`) ahead of the game's own script (`vite.config.ts`). Everything the game asks of where it runs goes through `src/engine/host.ts`: on YouTube, the player's save is loaded from their YouTube account before the game starts (`src/main.ts` waits for it, then loads `src/app.ts`) and sent back a moment after each change (at once when YouTube pauses the game); the game pauses when YouTube says so (a race goes to its pause screen) and is silent while YouTube's sound is off; it tells YouTube when its first frame is drawn and when it can be played (its first screen is up). There's no title splash (YouTube shows its own loading screen).
- **Nothing outside YouTube:** built without the backend, so no play stats, crash reports, REPORT, online boards or Daily Challenge board (the Daily Challenge is played as ever, your best kept, no initials asked for); no SHARE (its card links out); Capacitor (the Android app's bridge) swapped for a stand-in (`src/engine/notNative.ts`), as its browser code reaches for cookies and the page's visibility.
- **The checks** (`tools/youtube-bundle.py`): the initial bundle (all but the music, which streams in after) under 30 MiB (15 recommended), the whole under 250 MiB, no file over 30 MiB; the SDK's script first; no browser storage, Page Visibility API or code made from strings; no outside addresses but the SDK's. It says what's text only (XML namespaces, a paper three.js's shaders cite, and three.js's Timer's page-visibility hook, which only `Timer.connect` wires up and nothing calls). Today: 0.99 MiB before the game's ready, 8.5 MiB in all, a 7.8 MiB zip.

## Layout

```
index.html        The handheld column: game screen (#screen) + control deck (#deck)
src/
  main.ts         The entry: the host made ready (engine/host.ts: the YouTube build's save loaded), then app.ts
  app.ts          Routes in the page: the circuit menu, or the race on ?circuit=<id>, without page loads
  engine/         Controls, deck, layout, storage, TUNE panel, view types, driving physics (driving.ts),

                  car models, daylight, sprites, textures)
  f1/             The race, race control (raceControl.ts: wrecks, safety car, penalties), pit lane and stops (pits.ts), circuit menu,
                  circuit layouts (layouts.ts), circuit builder and scene, race rules and tuning; uses only engine/
    race.ts       The race view: its sessions, the loop (input, steps, events, sounds, camera) and the debug hooks
    race/         The race view's parts: the HUD and its layout (hud.ts), the readout's lines (readout.ts) and their rows on the
                  screen (readoutView.ts), the pixel icons (icons.ts), the banner
                  (banner.ts), the timing tower (towerView.ts), the results and qualifying tables (resultsView.ts), the
```

Saves live in local storage under the `cc:` prefix, as one versioned document, `cc:save` (`src/engine/save.ts`; the game's format in `src/f1/save.ts`): **settings** (sound, vibration, thumbstick side, layout), **choices** (the menu's last mode, circuit, team, difficulty, weather, qualifying and a Quick Race's laps), **ghosts** (your Time Trial record laps), **championship** (the season in progress), **progress** (the circuits unlocked), **records** and **circuits** (each circuit's hash when last played). Each value is checked when it's read, so a missing or odd one falls back to its default. A change of format bumps the version and adds a migration from the one before, and an older save is brought up to date on its first read (the separate keys of before the save format are carried over once, then removed). A save from a newer version of the game (an app rolled back) is read but never written over, and one that can't be read is set aside as `cc:save.bad` rather than lost. Every write re-reads the stored save and changes just its one value, so two tabs don't undo each other. The TUNE values (`cc:tune:f1`) and vehicle edits (`cc:vehicles`) are dev tools and keep their own keys. On a phone the race picture fills the screen top to bottom, with the overlay floating over it as laid out in the HUD Lab, everything centred or mirrored about the middle of the screen (the readout and the timing tower the same width either side, RESTART and PAUSE the same width in the corners, the minimap and your lap in the middle, A mirroring the thumbstick): RESTART and PAUSE (EXIT while paused) in the top corners with the minimap between them, the lap readout down the left (at the timing tower's size; LAP, LAST, BEST, REC, CAR and the tyres as blocks, without the gaps, which the tower has, or the tyres' percentage), the timing tower down the right, your lap (LAP 2/5, in a small panel like the tower's; your position is in the tower) under the minimap, the banner and then the team radio across the middle above the track, and the thumbstick in the bottom right corner (STICK in the settings moves it left), with A in the other corner when it has something to do; your car stays in the middle of the screen (`src/engine/layout.ts`'s `fitHandheld`, the readouts' places in `src/f1/race/hud.ts`). TUNE sits at the bottom between them.
